## Why it matters for an inspection

A home that installs rooftop solar uses less electricity from the grid during the day, so its meter
reading drops. On the meter data alone this can look like a bypass. Solar can only explain a daytime
drop: it produces nothing at night. A drop in night-time use, or a daytime drop larger than the
panels can produce, still needs an explanation, even when solar is confirmed.

## Solar ATAP

Solar ATAP (Solar Accelerated Transition Action Programme) started on 1 January 2026 and continues
the Net Energy Metering (NEM) programme. The owner installs panels for their own use; surplus energy
can be exported to the grid and offsets the energy charge on the bill. The meter is bidirectional,
so exported energy shows as export readings.

## NEM (Net Energy Metering)

NEM was introduced in 2016 and let owners offset their consumption with solar energy exported to the
grid. It closed to new applications on 30 June 2025. Homes registered under NEM keep their
registration and their bidirectional meter.

## SelCo (self-consumption)

Under SelCo the solar energy is used on site only and is not exported to the grid. The meter never
shows export readings: the only sign on the meter is lower daytime use from the grid. A SelCo home
without a registration on record therefore looks most like a bypass in the data.

## What to check on site

- Panels and inverter present; note the inverter's total generation reading (kWh).
- Compare the inverter's daily generation with the drop in metered use: the panels cannot explain a
  drop larger than they produce.
- Registration document seen: Solar ATAP, NEM or SelCo, with the installed capacity (kWp).
- Home battery present or not: a battery can move solar energy into the evening.
- Check the wiring before the meter for a bypass, even when solar is confirmed.
